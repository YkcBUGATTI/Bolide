DOC.01 2020 → 2021 — FROM CONCEPT TO PRODUCTION

The MeteoriteBOLIDE — LE MÉTÉORE

The name comes from 19th-century French — bolide: a meteor that crosses the atmosphere at speed and burns out on its way to earth. A fitting name for a machine built with one purpose and no mercy.

From a blank sheet to a drivable car took less than a year. Designers and engineers simulated maximum loads, acceleration and lap times in a virtual environment — then built it. In December 2020, the photo release carried a single sentence: The Bugatti Bolide is real.

At first it was "a drivable experimental vehicle." The enthusiasm of customers worldwide changed that. In August 2021, at The Quail in Monterey, Bugatti announced: the Bolide will be produced, limited to 40 units.

The recognition followed quickly. In 2021, an expert jury at the Festival Automobile International in Paris voted it the most beautiful hypercar in the world — by a substantial margin. In 2022, it took the "Design Award" at the Concorso d'Eleganza Villa d'Este on Lake Como.

The juries saw the same thing: every surface of this car is working. Not a single line exists for decoration — that is its beauty.

DOC.02 MOLSHEIM DESIGN STUDIO — FRANK HEYL

DesignFORM FOLLOWS PERFORMANCE

The design team engraved one creed into every line of the Bolide: every technical consideration has been translated directly into an aesthetic design.

Under hard braking the nose drops, the front splitter closes on the ground and downforce rises — yet the engineers deliberately cap the front axle's downforce to preserve aero balance. Air curtains ahead of the front wheels guide the flow around the car; winglets on the splitter's edges create vortices that feed the diffuser. Side intercoolers sit in deep intakes to hold the engine at its optimum temperature. Even the mirrors: Bugatti chose physical mirrors over cameras, because drivers judge distances faster.

The rear wing adapts to each circuit's character, balancing downforce against drag. Aerodynamic development of this kind usually exists only at the top level of motorsport. Here, it serves forty owners.

X — the signature Bugatti took from the X-planes of aviation history. Visible from every perspective.

X-shaped taillights frame the central exhaust; the theme runs from the nose through the cockpit to the seats. The name, the lights, the interior, the wheels — X is the Bolide's most direct identity mark, and the only "decoration" its design language allows.

It all begins with the design studio's sketches. These archival drawings — Frank Heyl's aerodynamic analysis, Aldo Sica's interior vision, Artur Hindalong's top-view layout — still mark where every technical decision landed.

Design and technology flow into one another in the Bolide. Every technical consideration has been translated directly into an aesthetic design.

DOC.04 8.0 L — QUAD TURBO — FIA LMH/LMDH

EngineeringTHE W16, UNLEASHED

The W16 — the heart of every road-going Bugatti for twenty years — is pushed to its final form in the Bolide. Race track optimization means recalculating everything.

Turbochargers and lubrication systems were redesigned to withstand higher temperatures and forces; intake and exhaust were de-throttled for faster response and a new W16 symphony. The engine sits in a newly developed carbon monocoque, positioned 60 mm further forward than in the Chiron — packaging built for the track.

The monocoque was developed to the demanding FIA LMH/LMDh requirements — the same standards as Le Mans prototypes — using a super-high-strength carbon material, the highest performing of its type in the world. Safety is not an add-on; it is part of the engineering.

The manufacturing is archival-grade, too.

A 3D-printed titanium pushrod weighs just 100 grams and transmits forces up to 3.5 tonnes; the X-shaped bracket in the cockpit, 3D-printed in aluminum, weighs 300 grams and withstands loads beyond 700 kg — precision as fine as 0.1 mm.

The brake system was developed with Brembo: carbon-carbon 390 mm discs on par with Formula 1 and LMh machinery, and the largest caliper Brembo has ever fitted to a racing car, with 8 front pistons and dedicated cooling ducts.

Before any Bolide meets its owner, it passes a final inspection and circuit testing program at Mirecourt — the first time Bugatti built its own quality process for a car with no benchmark.

To pass these stringent but critical safety tests, we needed to ensure that everything about the monocoque was technically perfect so that on the track the Bolide is a race car that is safe and secure in every driving situation and environment.
For a car so extreme that there really was currently no benchmark for it. So, we developed our own, accepting nothing less than perfection.
DOC.05 MONOCOQUE — LMP1 INSPIRED

The CockpitTHE COCKPIT

The cockpit draws its inspiration from LMP1 prototypes: reduced to driving.

Everything unrelated to driving was asked to leave — the wheel, the pedals, one essential screen, and the driver. The seats carry the X-theme in multi-pad stitching; at rest, the steering wheel becomes a piece of sculpture.

Even entering and leaving the cockpit was designed with care: the outer sections of the wraparound headrest fold away, letting the driver settle into the seat with composure.

The X continues in here.

The cockpit melds around the same daring X as the rear: seats, headrest brackets, door structures. The 3D-printed X bracket weighs 300 grams and carries more than 700 kg.

This interior is not "track-style luxury." It is restraint in service of driving — every material, every stitch exists so the driver forgets the interior exists, and remembers only the car.

At Monterey Car Week, the 100-year anniversary Bolide shared the asphalt with the car that inspired it — a century after the Type 35's first race. One blue, one black: two timelines reunited on the hills of Laguna Seca.

A US owner took his freshly delivered Bolide to the Circuit of the Americas — a Christmas gift to himself. It rained that day. The look on his face when he returned to the pits said everything.

"Feeling The Track" — the first official event

The largest gathering so far. At first light, eight meteorites lined up in the pit lane of the Miami International Autodrome — the W16's roar was the only language spoken.

This was the moment we had all been working toward — witnessing multiple Bolide examples unleashed on the track together.
DOC.07 40 UNITS — MOLSHEIM ATELIER

LegacyA VISION BECOMES LEGACY

November 2025. The final Bolide was completed at the Molsheim Atelier.

Finished in French Racing Blue with Nocturne Black Carbon across the lower body — a customer's choice, a tribute to Bugatti's racing history. Its completion closed the project: from the online reveal of 2020 to the last delivery, the W16's purest form was concluded in 40 units.

As the Chiron cycle came to an end, the Bolide and W16 Mistral became the last Bugattis powered by the W16. After the Bolide, this twenty-year-old engine enters history — in its most pure, most unsparing form.

For the owners, the journey was just beginning.

A global network of Certified Bolide Centers supports the car — from pre-delivery inspection and personalized handover to annual servicing, all carried out by teams trained to motorsport standards. The service bar is set as high as the car itself.

And the annual track program for owners — from Paul Ricard to Miami — is becoming a tradition of its own. Racing dreams are fulfilled here, and then they become stories.

The idea of being a perfect track car for both gentlemen and professional drivers is not so easy to translate into driving attributes, but it's essential to what makes it a Bugatti.
We set out to create a car that can perform on the racetrack yet also belongs in the world's finest collections.
